# Phase 03 — Backtest and Ledger Design

Status: planned. The current repository has no Phase 03 runner, simulator, financial ledger, replay store, or metric engine. This design specifies six separately authorized implementation slices; this documentation reconciliation implements none of them and makes no return, alpha, data-coverage, or readiness claim.

## Purpose and boundary

Phase 03 adds an offline, deterministic daily backtest over the sealed inputs produced by Phase 01 and the shadow-only decision envelope produced by Phase 02. It gives each decision a later, separately sealed outcome stream, simulates only precommitted fixture intents, records financial effects in an append-only ledger, and derives reproducible reports.

The Research Graph in `tradingagents/` remains an upstream-derived research namespace. No Phase 03 implementation edits its graph, cache, checkpointer, provider adapters, or runtime behavior. `TradingAgentsGraph._fetch_returns()` and `_resolve_pending_entries()` are research reflection/outcome paths, not portfolio accounting inputs. `load_ohlcv()` remains a current dataflow, not a sealed historical source. Production backtest modules consume typed production contracts and data; only `mytradingalpha.research` may adapt `tradingagents`. Backtrader remains unused, and this phase adds no dependency.

The existing production inputs are the immutable `RunContext`, typed-domain v1 `EvidenceBundle`, witnessed `TradingCalendar`, and full-source `SignalEnvelope`. Preserve their public imports, JSON-ingress denial, sealed bytes, IDs, and validation. The current `TradingCalendar.next_session(after: date | str)` takes an exchange-local date, not a `datetime`; it requires verified coverage and never infers weekends, holidays, or gaps. Calendar replay evidence is optional for legacy PIT readers. The new BT path requires the already sealed `CalendarReplayEvidence` at its own boundary and maps a decision instant through exactly one `CalendarReplayDay` interval `[start_utc, end_utc)`. Do not consult host `ZoneInfo` or current timezone data, or capture a witness during BT replay.

Phase 03 introduces no live or paper broker writes, credentials, approvals, scheduler, strategy allocator, risk decision, OMS lifecycle/outbox, or promotion behavior. Simulation plan and risk references are descriptive lineage only; `risk_decision_id` does not approve real risk. OMS dispatch must reject the BT-02 simulation-only v1 lineage. Any later authority extension requires its own versioned contract and explicit authorization; this phase defines or implements none. Synthetic tests establish software behavior only.

## BT-01 — Decision binding, clock, and events

The proposed immutable `SessionBinding` contains one exact `RunContext`, `EvidenceBundle`, and `SignalEnvelope`; the witnessed calendar comes from that sealed bundle. The runner accepts bounded tuples of already validated bindings and source data. It accepts no callback, iterator, provider, loader, model, lazy query, or caller-defined resolver. It revalidates and defensively snapshots nested source data at ingress, verifies the bundle/context/envelope/calendar/variant/instrument identities, and rejects a changed source snapshot.

For every binding, use the existing witness, not the machine's timezone database:

1. Find exactly one replay day whose UTC half-open interval contains `context.decision_time`, use its sealed `local_date`, then require the exact injected session and `context.decision_time == session.close_at`. Reject zero or multiple matching witness intervals.
2. Require `context.knowledge_cutoff <= context.decision_time`, and verify all decision-visible source availability and, for archive-realistic replay, ingestion by that cutoff.
3. Require `context.earliest_execution_time` to be at or after the open of the next verified eligible session returned by `TradingCalendar.next_session(session_date)`. Refuse a missing witness, missing session, coverage gap, end-of-coverage, mismatched calendar, or chronology that cannot be proved. Do not calculate weekdays or bridge a gap.
4. Preserve `SignalEnvelope.shadow_only`. A `no_trade` envelope still produces an explicit no-trade decision and deterministic next-session opportunity record, but it never authorizes a simulated action or a new fixture intent in BT-02. BT-01 never maps a signal to quantity or a portfolio target.

Decision inputs and future outcome evidence are different sealed records. A later final daily bar, its content, source payload, and hashes cannot enter an earlier decision binding. `DecisionEvent` contains the exact input references and cutoff. `OpportunityEvent` identifies the next verified session and permitted outcome window but carries no fill. Later outcomes reuse the existing `mytradingalpha.data.bars.DailyBar`, require `finality=FINAL` and `adjustment_basis=UNADJUSTED`, and bind exact instrument, calendar, session, source and revision. They must be available by the configured outcome cutoff and, for archive-realistic replay, ingested by the archive cutoff. A future, revised-late, provisional, adjusted, stale, wrong-source, or mismatched bar is rejected or yields a typed unavailable result; it is never silently substituted.

The total event sort key is `(economic session/time, fixed stage priority, stable instrument_id, content-stable event_id)`. Stage priority is fixed as opening action entitlement/identity, cash payment, eligible fill settlement, closing mark/NAV, decision, and future opportunity. BT-01 emits only decision and future-opportunity stages; later slices fill their reserved earlier stages without changing existing tie order. Caller collections are canonicalized by this key before sequence numbers are assigned. Duplicate or conflicting event identity, ambiguous chronology, unsupported stage, or changed input fails closed. The resulting sequence is contiguous from its declared start. Processing order and observation/availability time are recorded separately from economic time.


## BT-02 — Orders, fills, and bounded costs

BT-02 first defines shared `OrderIntent` and `Fill` in `mytradingalpha/contracts/orders.py`. The contract is strict, immutable, finite-Decimal, UTC-aware and bounded. It includes stable intent/fill IDs; run, instrument, decision, envelope and outcome-source references; currency; side; positive share quantity; market or positive-price limit type; earliest submission; finite expiry; TIF; simulation-only `plan_id`, `risk_decision_id`, and `risk_policy_version` lineage; and `execution_authority="simulation_only"`. Quantity is a positive magnitude and side supplies its sign. Market orders have no limit; limit orders have one positive limit. The Fill records the full intent link, actual positive filled quantity, all-in execution price, non-negative incremental explicit fee, reference price and versioned cost attribution, economic fill time, later observation/receipt time, source references and simulated provenance. Economic time is never rewritten to make a late outcome appear known earlier.

`SimOrder`, `OrderBook`, `Simulator`, and `FillModel` remain internal to `mytradingalpha/backtest/`. The shared contracts are not OMS records. They do not introduce broker IDs as authority, order-state transitions, outbox delivery, unknown acknowledgements, or dispatch. A `no_trade` envelope creates no new fixture intent. A test intent is frozen before its eligible outcome session and is not produced by mapping signal scores to quantity.

The initial `CostModel.quote()` stays importable from `mytradingalpha.backtest.costs`; later EXC slices extend that package facade without changing its public import. BT-02 requires an explicitly configured, versioned constant spread in basis points, slippage in basis points, per-share commission, order minimum, fixed-share per-instrument/session fill cap, and lot assumption. Zero cost is valid only when explicit in the policy. Missing, negative, non-finite, unsupported-currency, or out-of-bound values fail. Calibrated impact, ADV, measured liquidity, and market capacity are unavailable until EXC owns their evidence; they are never implicitly free.

For reference close `P > 0`, side sign `s` (`+1` buy, `-1` sell), half-spread basis points `h`, and slippage basis points `l`, baseline adverse all-in price is:

```text
execution_price = P + s * P * (h + l) / 10,000
```

For buys, a limit is marketable only when this adverse price is at or below the limit; for sells it is marketable only when it is at or above the limit. A non-marketable limit has no fill. The simulator does not cap the adverse price at the limit to hide friction. The baseline is retrospective next-session-close execution: the intent is fixed before the eligible session and uses its final close, never its open, high, low, intraday range, or an inferred path. Economic `fill_time` is that session close; `received_at`/resolution time records later outcome availability. A decision at that same close cannot observe an unresolved fill or late bar; its state uses only its own knowledge cutoff. Report accounting may include the retrospective fill after resolution, and is labeled separately from decision-visible state. There is no arbitrary same-bar exception.

The next verified eligible session close is the first attempt. At every attempt enforce earliest_submit_time and finite expires_at. Fill quantity is bounded by remaining intent quantity, available cash after the incremental fee, owned shares for a sale, remaining fixed-share cap and configured lot. The cap is consumed in stable intent-ID order and says nothing about measured market liquidity. Zero cap yields no fill. No shorting or leverage is allowed. DAY expires at the eligible session close after its attempt; GTC retries a non-marketable or partial remainder at each subsequent verified eligible session close until expiry; IOC makes one eligible attempt then cancels its remainder; FOK fills the entire remaining affordable and cap-eligible quantity or nothing. Missing outcomes do not become free fills.

For cumulative filled shares `Q_after`, per-share commission `c`, and order minimum `m`, fee due after the current fill is zero at `Q_after=0`, otherwise `USD 0.01` rounding with `ROUND_HALF_EVEN` applied to `max(m, c*Q_after)`. Incremental fee is `fee_due(Q_after) - fee_due(Q_before)`, where `Q_before = Q_after - current_fill_quantity`; charge the order minimum once, not on each partial. The current policy is USD-only; another currency requires its own explicit versioned quantum and rounding policy. Cost attribution totals are currency amounts for actual filled shares and are not an extra cash debit. Cash change for signed filled shares `q` and execution price `p` is `-q*p - incremental_fee`. Accounting examples and the fee-once rule remain compatible with [shared contract equations](../../03_CONTRACTS_AND_SCHEMAS.md#fill-accounting-units-and-fee-once-rule). The numeric magnitude/scale bounds and arithmetic context are fixed in the BT-02 JIT; 2-, 3-, and 5-partial sequences must reconcile exactly under this cumulative rule.




## BT-03 — Ledger and NAV

BT-03 owns immutable internal `LedgerEvent` and `LedgerBalance`, plus `Ledger.append()`, `Ledger.replay()`, `Ledger.balance()`, `NAVCalculator.compute()` and `AccountingInvariant.check()`. These are internal accounting records, not the future RSK `PortfolioSnapshot` or an OMS event. Opening capital is explicit USD simulation cash. External capital flows and FX are absent unless a separately reviewed contract later models them.

An accepted fill posting links the complete validated intent and fill and atomically records the signed position change, all-in notional cash change, incremental fee, and cumulative fee state. No half-fill or independently appendable cash/position leg exists. Ledger sequence is contiguous and content-hash chained. After validating bounded event identity and canonical payload, check the ID index first: identical known ID/payload returns the original event and balance as a no-op without a sequence check; changed payload under a known ID conflicts. Only a new ID must match the exact next sequence before invariants and arithmetic are checked and state commits. Any conflict, gap, invalid chronology, or failed arithmetic rejects before changing state. Retained events are immutable and returned defensively.

Every transition preserves long-only positions and non-negative cash. Reject an unaffordable buy or oversell; never round it into a successful fill. Use explicit receivable/liability creation and settlement events that balance both sides. `NAV = cash + Σ(shares × eligible unadjusted mark) + receivables − liabilities`. A missing, future, stale, wrong-session, wrong-instrument, or wrong-currency required mark makes NAV unavailable and the run invalid under the declared required-mark policy. No zero or last-current-mark fallback is allowed. Fee attribution and implementation shortfall are not debited again. Ledger arithmetic uses a local fixed Decimal precision/rounding policy independent of ambient process context; bounded magnitudes and scales make notional/addition exact, and only cumulative explicit fees are rounded to currency quantum. Report-only rounding cannot mutate ledger values.


## BT-04 — Corporate actions and benchmarks

BT-04 consumes existing point-in-time action records and does not rewrite PIT. Only the action revision available at its effective application boundary may be used. Late effective-action evidence invalidates the run or requires a fresh replay artifact; it cannot be inserted into a sealed history. All execution and marks use unadjusted prices plus explicit action accounting. Reject provider-adjusted prices combined with explicit actions.

At the opening action stage, the versioned same-session tie rule is split share conversion, stable-instrument identity update, then dividend entitlement. A split multiplies shares by its exact ratio and adjusts per-share cost basis/mark inversely so NAV is preserved before market movement and cost. It cancels outstanding pre-split intents with an explicit reason instead of silently resizing them. Fractional shares are retained exactly or settled with preregistered, evidenced cash-in-lieu; never truncate. Dividend entitlement uses holdings carried into the ex/effective session, after the declared same-session split conversion and before that session trades; the declared dividend amount is per post-split share. An amount stated on a pre-split or ambiguous basis requires an explicit conversion supported by action evidence; otherwise it is unavailable. Never apply a pre-split amount to post-split shares by assumption. Accrue the receivable exactly once. Payment transfers that receivable to cash on or after the declared pay date; if the pay date is not a session, a versioned witnessed-calendar rule schedules settlement on the next eligible session and never early.

Ticker aliases retain stable `instrument_id` and create no cash movement. A merger/conversion into a distinct instrument ID is unavailable unless explicit action evidence and a declared mapping exist. Delisting prevents further trading, cancels residual intents with a reason, and requires versioned, evidenced settlement or an explicit simulation writeoff. It never invents proceeds from the last observed price.

Cash and buy-and-hold (B&H) each own separate ledgers and NAV series with the same initial capital, calendar, currency, costs, and action policy as the strategy. Cash earns zero interest unless an explicit evidenced rate is configured. B&H freezes its initial instruments/weights, entry session, lots, residual cash, no-rebalance rule and dividend treatment; baseline dividends remain cash and are not reinvested. Neither benchmark can substitute for strategy NAV or hide a survivor-only universe.


## BT-05 — Replay, hashes, and local recovery

BT-05 owns a versioned `ReplayManifest`, typed semantic projections, and local `ReplayCheckpoint` persistence in the backtest replay boundary. The manifest binds every decision and outcome bundle content hash, policy and calendar witness, universe/instrument lineage, explicit variant and signal semantic source content, cached/model/config/code/schema versions, numerical/fill/cost/action/benchmark policies, initial balances, eligible session stream, seed, and all economic events/results. Upstream sealed v1 bytes, IDs, and exact hashes remain unchanged and are retained in the exact artifact integrity record.

Keep three domain-separated SHA-256 identities distinct:

1. `semantic_input_hash` identifies the typed, versioned economic input projection.
2. `economic_output_hash` binds `semantic_input_hash` and identifies the typed, versioned economic result projection; material input changes therefore change this output identity even if numeric values coincide.
3. `artifact_integrity_hash` covers the exact canonical artifact, including original sealed-source bytes/hash references and full operational/run lineage.

Economic projections omit only fields explicitly declared non-semantic by each known contract: `run_id`, correlation ID, wall-clock operational timestamps, and derived run-dependent IDs/hash fields. Exclusions are recursive only through those typed, versioned contract projections. Preserve all economic time and provenance: decision time, cutoff, source availability/ingestion, publication/event/effective/payment/fill time, order and ledger sequence, and semantic `created_at`; SIG-05 `created_at == decision_time` is material. Recompute projected semantic IDs and references consistently. Do not put an exact SIG/source integrity hash that depends on excluded run fields back into the economic projection; retain it in exact artifact provenance. Model/config/code versions, captured output content and all economic values remain material. There is no generic recursive key stripping.

Before canonicalization or hashing, reject unknown fields/schema versions, cycles, duplicate JSON keys, NaN/infinity, custom objects, mutable or ambiguous values, and payloads over the explicit resource bounds. Use canonical UTF-8 JSON with sorted mapping keys, preserved economic sequence, one documented exact Decimal string normalization, UTC timestamp serialization, and separate domain prefixes. Bounds and arithmetic/serialization versions are material inputs. Existing sealed bytes and upstream IDs are never rewritten.

A checkpoint binds both semantic and exact manifest inputs; committed ledger prefix count and hash; runner cursor; open orders; cumulative fees; remaining fill cap; action entitlements/payables; benchmark state; and reducer versions. The checkpoint is derived evidence; ledger events remain authoritative. Resume validates the exact manifest and event prefix, replays and reconciles state to the checkpoint, then resumes at the next unapplied event. Crash before an atomic fill commits nothing; a crash after event commit but before checkpoint replays the event once. Torn event, unmatched hash/sequence, conflict, changed input, unknown version, or unreconciled checkpoint stops and retains evidence.

There is one explicit local append owner. Publish each canonical event/artifact atomically under an explicit caller-supplied artifact root. Do not overwrite prior inputs/results. Reject symlink traversal, path escape, opaque object loaders and concurrent writers. File I/O is allowed only beneath that root; the Phase 03 path cannot discover environment credentials or invoke a provider, network, subprocess, or model. A semantic change creates a new derived artifact. Unchanged resume must match uninterrupted economic output exactly.

## BT-06 — Metrics and reports

Metrics are calculated from positive, aligned, unflowed end-session NAV series `V_0 … V_n`; benchmark values and risk-free returns use the same session/currency/net policy. A required series, mark, interval, or policy that is missing, misaligned, non-finite, non-positive where a ratio requires positivity, or unsupported makes the relevant metric `unavailable` with a stable reason. Never encode unavailable as zero, NaN, infinity, or a made-up fallback.

For interval returns `r_i = V_i / V_(i-1) − 1`, cumulative return is `V_n / V_0 − 1`. Let `A` be the explicitly versioned annual session count (252 is an assumption, never inferred from observed rows), and `D > 0` actual elapsed UTC days. Session-annualized return is `(V_n/V_0)^(A/n) − 1`; calendar CAGR is `(V_n/V_0)^(365.25/D) − 1` and is not labeled calendar CAGR when `D` is unavailable. Sample volatility is `sqrt(A) * sqrt(Σ(r_i−mean(r))²/(n−1))` for `n ≥ 2`.

With aligned risk-free return `rf_i`, excess return is `e_i = r_i − rf_i`. Sharpe is `sqrt(A)*mean(e)/sample_sd(e)`. Sortino is `sqrt(A)*mean(e)/sqrt(Σ(min(e_i,0)²)/n)`. Calmar is calendar CAGR divided by positive maximum drawdown. If elapsed-day CAGR or a positive denominator is unavailable, Calmar is unavailable; a zero or undefined denominator is never infinity.

Drawdown is `d_i = 1 − V_i/max(V_0 … V_i)` and maximum drawdown is `max(d_i)`. Drawdown duration counts consecutive unrecovered sessions and marks a terminal unrecovered period as censored. Active return is `r_i−b_i`; tracking error is `sqrt(A)*sample_sd(r−b)`; information ratio is `sqrt(A)*mean(r−b)/sample_sd(r−b)`. Beta is `sample_cov(r−rf,b−rf)/sample_var(b−rf)`. Descriptive arithmetic Jensen alpha is `A*(mean(r−rf)−beta*mean(b−rf))`; it makes no significance or validated-alpha claim. These require at least two aligned observations, positive benchmark variance for beta, and matching currency, calendar, fees and benchmark policy. Relative drawdown uses relative wealth `(V_i/V_0)/(B_i/B_0)`.

One-way session turnover is `Σ(abs(q_filled)*reference_price)/(2*opening_NAV)`, including initial purchases. Annualized turnover is `A*mean(session_turnover)` over all eligible sessions, including no-trade sessions. Gross exposure is `Σ(abs(q)*mark)/NAV`; net exposure is `Σ(q*mark)/NAV`; cash weight is `cash/NAV`; long-only leverage bound and maximum instrument concentration are explicit. Total spread/slippage attribution and explicit fees use actual fills. Impact remains unavailable. Implementation shortfall includes explicit fees once. Gross performance is available only as an explicitly labeled same-fill-schedule counterfactual using reference prices and zero fees; without that trace it is unavailable. Do not add cost back to daily net returns or call it a strategy rerun under free fills.

Report fill count separately from closed round-trip lots. Hit rate and holding period require declared FIFO lot matching; otherwise they are unavailable with a stable reason. Intent count and filled-quantity rate are measurable only from eligible simulation intents and fills; they do not imply risk rejection or broker acknowledgement. Capacity, ADV, impact, missing-PIT-sector exposure, DSR, PBO, confidence/significance, validated alpha, and promotion fields are explicitly unavailable until their EXC/RSK/EXP/OMS/FWD owners supply evidence.

For `m` valid comparable run values sorted ascending, linear quantile uses `h=(m−1)p` and interpolation between `floor(h)` and `ceil(h)`; median uses `p=.5`, p5 `.05`, and p95 `.95`. A report states `m`, total runs, failed run IDs/reasons, cohort identity checks, and direction-aware worst values (minimum return/Sharpe; maximum loss/drawdown/cost). Failed or incomparable runs are never silently omitted or treated as EXP qualification. With one valid run, distribution sufficiency is unavailable rather than a robust headline. Each metric record has `status=available|unavailable`, nullable `value`, stable `reason`, units, formula version and sample count. The BT-06 JIT fixes Decimal root/power precision and final serialization quantum before RED; report calculations remain deterministic and do not mutate the ledger.

## Integrated Phase 03 acceptance

The final BT-06 integrated fixture uses at least two instruments; a holiday and an early close; a no-trade envelope; a precommitted next-session-close intent; marketable and non-marketable limits; 2-, 3-, and 5-partial fee sequences; explicit spread, slippage, minimum fee and fixed-share cap; atomic cash/position/NAV accounting; a split followed by ex-date and pay-date dividend with declared post-split per-share amount basis; ticker alias and both missing and explicit delisting settlement; separate Cash and B&H ledgers; uninterrupted and crash/checkpoint/resume paths; and aligned golden metrics plus unavailable later-phase fields.

The acceptance result is pass, fail, or insufficient_evidence. A phase-level pass requires all six BT PRs merged in order, exact-head independent review and required CI for each, and successful required golden runs. Expected negative-case rejections and documented later-phase unavailable metrics must pass their assertions and do not themselves block software acceptance. An unexpected failure in a required successful golden run or a failed required check blocks the gate; missing artifacts or checks are insufficient_evidence. Failed or incomparable experiment runs remain disclosed and block later EXP qualification, but are never silently omitted. Synthetic fixture success proves software behavior only; it does not establish genuine historical source coverage, economic realism, alpha, capacity, readiness for Phase 04/05, or authorization for paper/live activity. This docs reconciliation leaves the phase planned and does not authorize BT-01.
